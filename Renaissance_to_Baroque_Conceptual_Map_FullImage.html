Sub Epoch 3: Crisis, Turmoil & Regulation

Late Renaissance, Reformation & Counter Reformation

During the Late Renaissance (Mannerism), harmonious balance gave way to compositions reflecting the religious turmoil between Protestants and the Catholic Church. In response, the Catholic met to strictly regulate how sacred subjects could be displayed in art.

Late Renaissance & Religious Regulation

When Paolo Veronese painted a lavish, crowded banquet for his Last Supper, Church inquisitors ordered him to change the title to Feast in the House of Levi to comply with Counter Reformation rules of decorum.

Key Concept: Art was closely policed to ensure theological clarity and prevent secular distractions in sacred scenes.

Paolo Veronese, Feast in the House of Levi / Last Supper (1573)
Oil on canvas: complex theatrical composition scrutinized by the Counter Reformation

Subsequent Era: c. 1600s to 1750

The Baroque Period: Motion, Emotion & Grandeur

Following the Renaissance from the to , the Baroque era unfolded amid permanent division between the Roman Catholic Church and Protestants, alongside an age of global exploration, trade, and discovery. Artists created theatrical scenes bursting with dynamic , intense , dramatic light and shadow, and imperial grandeur.

Visualizing Drama & Discovery

Whether serving Catholic Counter Reformation devotion through visceral realism or celebrating Protestant Dutch global trade and wealth, Baroque art pulled the viewer directly into the action.
